The premise

This is not a true‑crime story.

Every prior account of Jeffrey Epstein begins at the end, with the girls, the flight logs and the famous names, and works backward without ever reaching an origin. Jeffrey Epstein Was A CIA Asset starts at the true beginning.

It traces the covert intelligence and financial apparatus the United States built during World War II, how it was funded outside congressional oversight for eight decades, and how a boy from a working-class family in Brooklyn was identified, groomed and deployed as one of its most valuable financial and blackmail operatives.

Epstein is not the story. He is the instrument through which the apparatus becomes visible.

This machine did not die in a jail cell on August 10, 2019. It is still running.

The evidence

Every claim, graded.

The book does not ask for trust. It grades every claim by the strength of its source, so the reader always knows what is documented and what is argued.

CAT 1

Primary source

The documents themselves: court filings, congressional reports, declassified records, financial and corporate filings.

CAT 2

Secondary source

Published accounts and testimony, weighed for reliability and flagged with their caveats.

CAT 3

Analytical inference

Reasoned conclusions, labeled as inference and never dressed up as established fact.

Where the record stops, the book says so, and it lists the claims it does not make.

It was the thread. This book is what he found when he pulled it.

The author

The operator behind the curtain.

Jason Sullivan has spent more than a decade where American politics rarely looks: behind the screens, inside the data and out of the spotlight. An investigator and open-source intelligence operator, he is the author of Jeffrey Epstein Was A CIA Asset.

He entered campaign politics in 2014. In 2016 he was a social media consultant to Roger Stone’s pro-Trump super PAC, working with software he built himself to spot what was about to go viral before it did.

That work brought Special Counsel Robert Mueller to his door. In 2018 Mueller subpoenaed him, and Mueller’s team interrogated him over whether he had acted as a go-between for Roger Stone and WikiLeaks founder Julian Assange. The next day he testified before a federal grand jury in Washington. He was never charged.

After January 6, 2021, investigators came again. In 2022 an investigative counsel for the House January 6th Committee interviewed him. The committee’s final report never named him, and he was never referred for prosecution.

Through both investigations The New York Times and Rolling Stone wrote about him, and so did more than a dozen other national outlets, from MSNBC and CNN to The Wall Street Journal, Reuters, ABC News, CBS News and Bloomberg. He gave them almost nothing. His lawyers answered for him. With his name in national headlines he could have spent those years on cable news. He chose to stay behind the curtain and keep working.

Since June 2022 he has been Chief Investigator at The Ticktin Law Group, working for Peter Ticktin, President Trump’s longtime friend and his Florida counsel in Trump v. Clinton. He played a pivotal role in the January 6 evidence effort that Ticktin says the President put him in charge of. His real-time internet sting captured January 6 posts before they vanished from Twitter, work featured by The Epoch Times. He also worked closely with award-winning journalist Lara Logan on her January 6 series, The Rest of the Story.

After years of saying almost nothing in public, he is ready to tell what he found.
